# Deal Health inspection rubric

An original-wording adaptation of the Selling at AI Speed workshop reference. It guides review; it is not an official Salesforce stage policy or an assertion about an individual customer's contract.

## Rating and evidence are different

- **Green:** the relevant claim is directly supported and confirmed; corroborate consequential approval/commitment claims where possible.
- **Amber:** useful but incomplete, second-hand, one-sided, stale or not yet tested evidence.
- **Red:** a consequential unknown, contradiction or structural gap requiring attention.

Alongside the colour record **confirmed / partial / unknown / contradicted**, so missing evidence is never described as customer rejection. Exact excerpts must be traceable to real input; where none exists, state “no evidence found in the reviewed sources”. Friendly email and an internal “committed” field do not justify a Green economic buyer or champion.

| Dimension | What we need to establish | Common trap | Useful next question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metrics | Customer outcome, measurable baseline, owner and target; distinguish target from achieved result | Treating utilisation or a proposal's ROI as realised value | What measure matters, who owns it and what baseline can we inspect? |
| Economic Buyer | Budget authority for this specific decision and evidence of access/engagement | Assuming the old contract's signatory or technical lead owns expansion spend | Whose budget funds this decision, and how do we confirm approval? |
| Decision Criteria | Customer-confirmed technical, operational, governance and commercial criteria | One enthusiastic user's feature list treated as the committee's criteria | Whose criteria have we not yet heard, including IT/security? |
| Decision Process | Actual steps, decision owners, dependencies and dates | Seller quarter-end target treated as a buying process | What must happen, in what order, before a decision can be made? |
| Paper Process | Applicable procurement, legal, security and signature mechanics with lead times | Assuming the previous contract or entitlement removes all approval steps | Which reviews remain for this scope, who owns them and how long do they take? |
| Identify Pain | Specific customer problem, consequence, urgency and affected owner | Renewal momentum or generic efficiency treated as compelling need | What happens if nothing changes, and to whom? |
| Champion | Influence and an observed action that advances the buyer's process | A friendly, responsive contact assumed to sell internally for us | What has this person actually done, and what influence has been tested? |
| Competition | Confirmed alternatives, status quo, reduced scope and customer criteria | “No named competitor” treated as no alternative | What else could solve this, including doing less or doing nothing? |

A renewal can be sound without an expansion. Evaluate the actual customer objective; do not downgrade it solely because there is no additional product to sell. For consumption-based agreements, actual utilisation and its definition may be useful evidence; it does not by itself prove value, budget or commercial rights.

## Original workshop review flags

The source rubric uses the following coaching conventions. State when using them, and let current team policy and actual deal evidence take priority:

- Evidence older than **45 days** prompts a freshness check; stable facts may remain valid, recent unsupported claims remain weak.
- A target decision within **60 days** with an unmapped paper process deserves explicit inspection. Compute against the stated as-of date and distinguish seller target from buyer commitment.
- More than **40% unknown roles** is a coverage flag only when the committee-role inventory and denominator are defined. Otherwise report the actual known relationships and unknown roles without a fabricated percentage.

These are not statistical predictions. A strong champion can still leave the seller dependent on one person. An expected role does not prove that a specific individual blocks the deal.

| Person / known role | Role in this decision | Stance | Relationship | Evidence / date | Gap or proposed introduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| … | Evidenced or unconfirmed | Champion / Supporter / Neutral / Blocker / Unknown | Strong / thin / unknown | … | … |

List expected but unidentified roles separately. In a diagram use solid lines for evidenced strong contact and dotted lines for thin/unknown paths. Label inferred edges.
