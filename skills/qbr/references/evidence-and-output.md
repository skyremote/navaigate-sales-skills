# QBR evidence worksheet and output

## Establish the scope

Record account identity, audience, period start/end, comparison period, time zone, as-of date, and the sources actually reviewed. If a report arrives after the quarter, distinguish its extraction date from the period its events cover.

## Metric table

| Metric | Definition, population and unit | Review period | Comparison period | Calculation / evidence | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meaningful metric | Event threshold, eligible population and exclusions | Counts, rate and dates | Matching counts, rate and dates | Input values, formula, source locator | Missing scope, changed definition or unknown |

When comparing two rates, calculate each from the appropriate denominator. Example: 30/50 = 60%; 18/40 = 45%; difference = 15 percentage points. That does not establish improvement among a fixed set of users, because the eligible population changed. A relative increase of 33.3% describes the rate, while an increase of 66.7% describes the active count; neither proves business value.

For a claim such as “saves time”, identify the measured task, baseline, later observation, sample, units and who validated it. If only an estimate exists, show its assumptions and label it as an estimate. Do not turn a hypothetical ROI scenario into an achieved result.

## Source and contradiction ledger

| Ref | Source, owner and date | Link or precise uploaded-file locator | Supports | Conflict or coverage gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Source title; effective/extraction dates where different | Real link, filename + worksheet/cell, or page/message | Claim or metric | Competing definition, partial period or missing evidence |

Keep locators usable by the reviewer. Do not invent URLs or copy unnecessary personal data into the review. An appendix with full private email bodies is rarely needed.

## One-page executive brief

**Account / period / as-of / audience**

**Customer objective:** the agreed business goal and success measure, with a source. If there is no agreed measure, make agreeing one an action.

**Evidence of progress:** two or three conclusions supported by measured outcomes and adoption evidence. Distinguish the two. State the most material limitation next to each conclusion.

**What needs attention:** unresolved commitments, important service issues, risks and disputed evidence. Show the customer consequence where evidenced; label proposed explanations.

**Decisions for this review:** the specific choices, support or agreement needed from the customer and our team.

## Joint 90-day plan

| Action and intended customer outcome | Owner | Due date | Success measure | Dependency / status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small, inspectable next step | Confirmed owner or proposed owner | Agreed date or proposed date | Observable measure and evidence source | Known blocker; agreed or proposed |

The plan spans the next 90 days; not every action should be due on day 90. Actions inferred by the assistant are proposals until agreed. Track outstanding actions from the prior review separately from newly proposed work.

## Optional slide outline

Use only the slides needed for this customer:

1. Customer objective and the decision needed today.
2. Progress against agreed outcomes, with evidence and gaps.
3. Adoption or workflow changes, using comparable measures.
4. Issues that affect the customer and what is being done.
5. Joint next-90-day plan and decisions.

For each slide specify one main message, the source, an appropriate visual and the question to discuss. Keep metric qualifications visible. Use an approved brand template when one is supplied. File generation, render review and publication are separate completion states.
